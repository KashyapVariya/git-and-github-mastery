## Why it matters

Both buttons sit near the top of every repository page, and it's easy to assume they do the same thing. They don't — one is a bookmark, the other is a subscription.

## Starring

Clicking "Star" adds the repository to your personal list at `github.com/your-username?tab=stars`, and adds one to that repository's public star count — a rough, widely used signal of popularity and community interest. Starring does nothing else: no notifications, no extra access. It's purely a bookmark plus a small public vote of appreciation.

## Watching

"Watch" controls what notifications you get from a repository — new issues, pull requests, releases, and discussions, depending on the watch level you choose:

- **Participating and @mentions** (the default for most repos) — only notifies you about things you're directly involved in or tagged in
- **All Activity** — every issue, PR, comment, and push notification the repo generates
- **Ignore** — mutes it completely, even if you're mentioned
- **Custom** — pick specific event types (releases only, for example)

<Callout type="tip">
Watching "All Activity" on a busy repository can flood your notifications fast. For projects you just want to stay loosely aware of, "Releases only" via Custom is often the better fit.
</Callout>

<Callout type="golden">
Starring a repository does not watch it, and watching a repository does not star it — set each one independently based on what you actually want: a bookmark, notifications, or both.
</Callout>

## Try it yourself

Find a project you use regularly, star it, then check its watch settings and set them to something that matches how closely you actually want to follow it.
